\section{Experimental design and data}\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Experimental design}

We conducted a randomized controlled trial (RCT) with agricultural extension agents (AEAs) in ten districts and irrigation schemes targeted by JICA's Ghana Rice Production Improvement Project (GRIP): eight rainfed rice-producing districts and two irrigation schemes.

We randomized at the district level. AEAs within the same district are each responsible for several communities (five to eight in our sample), and their assigned communities usually do not overlap. Nevertheless, they all report to the same district director and commute to the same district office, so randomization at the AEA level could cause the intervention effects to spill over to AEAs in the control group. We randomly assigned the eight rainfed districts to treatment group 1 (three districts), treatment group 2 (three districts), and the control group (two districts). In addition, we introduced a cross-cutting GPS treatment (described below) to a randomly selected half of the AEAs in each district. Because the production environment of the two irrigation schemes differs substantially from that of the rainfed areas, the schemes were excluded from the randomization and received no treatment. Although we also surveyed the irrigation schemes, we do not use these data; the remainder of this paper covers the rainfed areas only. Figure~\ref{fig:map} presents the study sites and their assigned treatment status.

We conducted the baseline survey in February 2025. All 42 AEAs in the eight district offices participated. The AEA questionnaire covered individual characteristics and career history (age, gender, education, civil-service grade, and tenure), work motivation (intrinsic and extrinsic), locus of control over agricultural production and their own career as well as in general, technical knowledge of recommended rice cultivation practices, self-reported extension effort (the number of farmer groups served and the frequency of visits), job satisfaction, and participation in training. We also surveyed 378 randomly selected rice farmers in the communities assigned to each AEA. Farmers were asked about household demographics and assets, rice production on their representative plot (area, output, and sales), input use and costs, family and hired labor by farm operation, the adoption of recommended management practices, land tenure, and their contact with and satisfaction with their AEA. We conducted the endline survey between January and February 2026. Apart from minor modifications, the endline questionnaires were identical to those of the baseline but asked about performance in the 2025 season.

\subsection{Interventions}

Following the baseline survey, we introduced three treatments for the corresponding AEAs from mid-April to the end of September 2025, a period that covers the rice production season.

\textbf{Feedback}: The first intervention is a bi-weekly feedback report based on farmers' evaluations of their AEA's service. From the second half of April to the end of September 2025, we conducted a brief bi-weekly phone survey of all sample farmers. The questionnaire covered (1) effort---whether the AEA had visited the community or the farmer in the past two weeks and, if so, how many times; (2) satisfaction---how satisfied the farmer was with the AEA's service in the past two weeks; and (3) the reason for that satisfaction or dissatisfaction. Based on the responses of each AEA's sample farmers (around ten farmers per AEA), we produced a one-page report in PDF format for AEAs in both treatment groups only and shared it with each AEA within two weeks. Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:feedback} shows examples of the feedback report. This treatment aims to enhance AEAs' intrinsic motivation through social recognition.

\textbf{Training}: We invited the 15 AEAs in treatment group 2 to an overnight sensitization training, held in April 2025 at a hotel in Koforidua, the capital of the Eastern Region of Ghana. The training was delivered by two assistant directors of the Directorate of Agricultural Extension Services (DAES) of the Ministry of Food and Agriculture (MoFA). It contained no technical training on rice production; instead, it focused on the social impacts and importance of agricultural extension. All 15 invited AEAs attended. This treatment aims to strengthen AEAs' locus of control over their extension services. Similar interventions have been shown to be effective in other public services, for example among community health workers in Pakistan \citep{khan_mission_2025}.

\textbf{GPS stamp}: We asked 20 randomly selected AEAs across the eight districts to submit their GPS coordinates through a KoboToolbox form whenever they visited farmers. This ``light'' monitoring treatment requires no special device other than a smartphone, and AEAs cannot manipulate the recorded location. Between mid-April and the end of September, 19 of the 20 AEAs submitted at least one record, and 357 records were submitted in total.

\textbf{Fuel support}: Between April and August 2025, we provided 150 Ghana cedis (less than US\$15 at the 2025 exchange rate) through mobile money to all AEAs. This support was given to both the treatment and control arms to cover fuel for their motorbikes. A former bilateral project had provided similar support to all district offices in previous years, but it was phased out in early 2025. We introduced comparable support for the AEAs in our sample district offices to minimize changes in the extension environment between the baseline and endline periods.
